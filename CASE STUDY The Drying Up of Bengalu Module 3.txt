CASE STUDY: The Drying Up of Bengaluru’s Lakes
Q1. Why were lakes so important for Bengaluru in the past?
  
   Bengaluru’s lakes were very important because the city did not have a major natural river flowing through it. Historically, people developed a network of interconnected lakes and tanks to collect and store monsoon rainwater. These lakes helped provide water for drinking, irrigation, and domestic activities. They also allowed water to slowly seep into the ground, helping with groundwater recharge. The interconnected lake system helped transfer excess rainwater from one lake to another and reduced flooding. Thus, lakes played an important role in supporting Bengaluru’s water supply, agriculture, ecosystem, and daily life.

Q2. What are the major reasons for the decline of Bengaluru’s lakes?

The major reasons for the decline of Bengaluru’s lakes are:

Rapid urbanisation: Expansion of buildings, roads, industries, and other infrastructure has reduced the natural areas available for lakes and wetlands.

Encroachment: Some lake areas and their catchment regions have been occupied for construction and other activities.

Sewage pollution: Untreated or inadequately treated sewage has entered lakes, causing severe water pollution and damaging aquatic ecosystems.

Dumping of solid waste: Garbage and construction waste have been dumped into and around several lakes.

Loss of interconnected drainage channels: Natural stormwater channels connecting lakes have been blocked or altered.

Reduction of catchment areas: Increased concrete surfaces prevent rainwater from naturally entering lakes and recharging groundwater.

Poor maintenance: Lack of regular desilting, removal of invasive plants, and protection of lake boundaries has contributed to deterioration.

As a result, many lakes have become polluted, reduced in size, or turned into degraded wetlands.

Q3. What lessons can cities learn from Bengaluru’s experience? Suggest measures to restore and protect urban lakes.
Bengaluru’s experience shows that urban development and water conservation must be planned together. Traditional water systems should not be abandoned without considering their importance to the city's water cycle. Protecting lakes can also help reduce flooding, improve groundwater recharge, support biodiversity, and provide recreational spaces.

Measures to restore and protect urban lakes:
Prevent encroachment by clearly identifying and protecting lake boundaries.

Treat sewage before discharge so that untreated wastewater does not enter lakes.

Restore interconnected stormwater channels between lakes.

Desilt lakes where necessary to increase their water-holding capacity.

Control solid-waste dumping and maintain clean surroundings.

Protect lake catchment areas so that rainwater can reach the lakes naturally.

Increase rainwater harvesting in homes, institutions, and commercial buildings.

Create buffer zones with suitable vegetation around lakes to reduce pollution and soil erosion.

Regularly monitor water quality and lake conditions.

Encourage community participation through lake-cleaning programmes and environmental awareness.

Integrate lake conservation into urban planning before approving new developments.

Conclusion

     Bengaluru’s lakes are an important part of the city’s water cycle, groundwater system, biodiversity, and flood management. Their decline demonstrates the environmental problems that can occur when rapid urbanisation takes place without adequate protection of natural water systems. Restoring lakes, treating sewage, protecting catchment areas, harvesting rainwater, and involving local communities can help create a more sustainable and water-secure city.
